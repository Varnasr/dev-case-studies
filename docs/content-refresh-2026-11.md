# Content refresh, November 2026

This file records a refresh of the case studies carried out on 21 November 2026. Every source named here was opened on that date and the passage relied on was read. Where a figure, date or quotation could not be traced to an opened source it was left out, and the gaps are listed at the end.

## Scope and method

- All 208 studies were triaged from the master list (period, topic, evidence tier). About 60 were then read or searched in the text for dated figures, programme status and the newest evidence cited.
- 28 studies were changed. 23 more were examined and left as they were. The rest were not examined beyond the triage.
- Sources used: programme and government statistics and releases, budget documents, World Bank, IMF, WTO, UN and UNEP pages and reports, national statistical offices, and peer-reviewed papers read through their PubMed records or journal pages. KFF fact sheets are used for the dated sequence of US global health policy actions in 2025 and 2026.
- Old figures were kept beside new ones where the trend matters, and every figure carries its year.
- New citations follow the style of the study they were added to. The em dash that older "Further Reading" entries use before their annotation is not used in the new entries.

## Is this file published?

`CLAUDE.md` does not mention a `docs/` folder. The site is served by GitHub Pages from the repository, with no deploy workflow that selects files and no `.nojekyll` file, so this file is likely to be published under `/dev-case-studies/docs/` (as rendered HTML if Pages runs Jekyll). Nothing links to it. `link-check.yml` crawls `**/*.md`, so every URL below will be checked on its daily run. Two hosts cited here (imf.org and unep.org) answered 403 to a plain HTTP client during this work and were read through a browser-based fetch; if the crawl reports them, that is the cause.

## Evidence tier

One study changed tier. `india-swachh-bharat` moved from emerging to moderate because it now cites Chakrabarti et al. (2024), a district panel study with two-way fixed effects that its authors describe as quasi-experimental. The library now has 7 strong, 60 moderate and 141 emerging studies, and 998 references (959 before).
